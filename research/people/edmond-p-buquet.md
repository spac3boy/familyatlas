### Family seafood-industry context, not Edmond's occupation

Aubin Vincent Buquet began drying shrimp on Terrebonne Parish marsh platforms in the early 1930s, shipped dried shrimp in wooden barrels to Japan, and opened Buquet Canning Co. in 1938 after the overseas market was disrupted. After Aubin's 1953 death, A.J. and other children continued the canning business and added oysters. A.J. became company president, represented seafood interests in state and federal fisheries matters, and testified before the U.S. Senate; Eve worked at the company. These activities are established for the family and named relatives. Although one business history says A.J. continued the company “along with his brothers and sisters,” it does not identify Edmond individually or state his role. This packet therefore does **not** assign Buquet Canning employment, ownership, or an occupation to Edmond.

## Biographical narrative

Edmond P. “Bud” Buquet was born on 16 February 1919 and died in November 1986. Michael identifies him as his paternal grandfather, says he was from Dulac, and remembers that he spoke Cajun French. A death-derived directory gives Houma as Edmond's last residence. These facts place him within Terrebonne Parish's bayou communities but do not establish a birthplace, a dated move from Dulac to Houma, or a death place.

Independent family evidence identifies Edmond as a son of Aubin Vincent Buquet and Lena Maronge Buquet, a brother of C. Robert, Aubin Joseph, Eve Marie, May, and Daisy, and the husband of Verna Arlene Bakke Buquet. Michael supplies Aubin Buquet as Edmond's son and his own father; obituary evidence independently places Aubin within Verna's child group and Edmond's wider family, although an original parentage record was not inspected.

The family's documented public history centers on Aubin Vincent's seafood-processing business in Dulac and the later work of Edmond's siblings, especially A.J. and Eve. That is important family context, but no reviewed source identifies Edmond's own occupation or confirms that he worked for Buquet Canning Co. The same evidence discipline applies to military history: Robert's WWII Navy service and Verna's WWII Navy nursing service are real but belong to them, not Edmond.

The direct working surname line continues, with decreasing source strength, from Edmond to Aubin Vincent, Quentin Alcide Augustin, François Luc Théodore, and France-born François Michel Jacques Bouquet/Buquet. The last man was in Louisiana by the births of his children and is probably the François Buquet documented on Bayou Terrebonne in 1825–1826. His exact French birthplace and parents were not found. The earlier suggestion of Saint-Malo was explicitly retracted in the original research.

## Conflicts and discrepancies

1. **Edmond versus Edmund.** Michael used `Edmund Buquet` once when introducing Verna, but his initial message, confirmation, and independent records use `Edmond`; `Edmond P. Buquet` is canonical. The `Edmund` form remains visible as a family-message discrepancy.
2. **Middle initial P.** The initial appears in independent records, but no source expands it. No middle name is invented.
3. **Dulac versus Houma.** Michael says Edmond was from Dulac; a death-derived result gives Houma as last residence. Both are within Terrebonne Parish and can describe different times or levels of association. Neither is asserted as birthplace or death place.
4. **C. Robert versus Robert Claude.** Eve's obituary calls Edmond's brother `C. Robert Buquet`; other project evidence uses `Robert Claude Buquet`. They are probably the same man, but the name order/expansion has not been confirmed by an original birth record.
5. **Quentin's name forms.** The research uses `Quentin Alcide Augustin Buquet`, `Quentin/Alcide Augustin Buquet`, and a cemetery transcription for a child uses `Alcide Iventin Augustin Buquet`. The first is retained as the working canonical form; `Iventin` is a material conflicting transcription, not silently normalized.
6. **Buquet versus Bouquet.** Earlier Louisiana compiled records alternate between the forms, particularly for François Michel Jacques, Marie Louise, and other children. Both are retained; the packet does not assume every Bouquet is part of this family.
7. **François Michel's birth year.** Compiled results use about 1785 and 1786. The packet preserves `about 1785/1786` and does not choose an exact year.
8. **François Michel's birthplace.** Early results asserted Saint-Malo or its vicinity. Later review could corroborate only France and explicitly retracted Saint-Malo. France is the probable broad place; the specific locality is unresolved.
9. **François Buquet in the 1825–1826 land record.** The name, time, and place fit François Michel Jacques, but the transcription omits middle names and family relationships. The identity match remains probable.
10. **Cajun identity versus Acadian paternal surname.** Michael's family identity and language history strongly support a Cajun cultural conclusion. That does not prove Buquet itself was an original Acadian surname or that François Michel was Acadian. Savoie and LeBlanc provide plausible Acadian lines, but their pedigrees were not completed in this research.

## Rejected hypotheses and false leads

### Saint-Malo as François Michel Jacques Buquet's established birthplace

Early compiled genealogy placed François Michel near Saint-Malo around 1785, and the timing appeared attractive because Saint-Malo housed Acadian refugees. The later evidence review could establish only France, not Saint-Malo. The precise place was expressly retracted and must not become family lore without a French or Louisiana original record naming it.

Evidence: `SRC-EDMOND-FRANCOIS-GENEALOGY`, `SRC-EDMOND-RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS`.

### François Michel as a passenger in the 1785 Acadian migration

The famous 1785 migration primarily departed from Nantes, and no passenger or sacramental record tied François Michel to it. Similar timing and a possible Saint-Malo origin were never proof. No ship, route, arrival date, or Acadian status is assigned to him.

Evidence: `SRC-EDMOND-FRANCOIS-GENEALOGY`, `SRC-EDMOND-CAJUN-CONTEXT`, `SRC-EDMOND-RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS`.

### Buquet as an original Acadian surname

The project found Savoie and LeBlanc in established Acadian surname/history sources, but not Buquet. The most responsible working interpretation is that the Buquet paternal line may have been French/Breton or from another French population and became culturally Cajun through long Louisiana residence and intermarriage. Even that origin model remains historical interpretation until François Michel's records are found.

Evidence: `SRC-EDMOND-SURNAME-HISTORY`, `SRC-EDMOND-CAJUN-CONTEXT`, `SRC-EDMOND-RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS`.

### Descent from Louis Léopold or Charles Joseph Buquet

The Napoleonic generals belonged to a documented family centered in Charmes, Vosges/Lorraine. The Louisiana immigrant's exact French origin is unresolved, and no common ancestor links the branches. Direct descent is especially implausible from the known titled line; a distant collateral relationship is merely possible in the abstract. Neither is included in Edmond's genealogy.

Evidence: `SRC-EDMOND-NAPOLEONIC-BUQUETS`, `SRC-EDMOND-RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS`.

### Seafood-company work or ownership assigned to Edmond

Buquet Canning Co. was founded by Edmond's father and continued by A.J. and other children; Eve is individually documented as an employee. No source names Edmond's position, work, or ownership. The company remains family history, not Edmond's occupation.

Evidence: `SRC-EDMOND-ASPA-BUSINESS`, `SRC-EDMOND-EVE-OBITUARY`, `SRC-EDMOND-AJ-POLICY`.

### Military records transferred from relatives

Robert Claude/C. Robert Buquet's WWII Navy MM2 record and Verna's Navy nurse service do not establish any service by Edmond. No military claim is made for him.

Evidence: `SRC-EDMOND-ROBERT-VETERAN`, `SRC-EDMOND-VERNA-OBITUARY`.

## Recommendations for future research

1. **Obtain Edmond's Louisiana birth/baptism record — highest priority for Edmond himself.** It should resolve birthplace, the middle initial, and parents with an original record.
2. **Locate Edmond's obituary, death certificate, and funeral-home file.** These are the best sources for exact death date/place, burial, occupation, military service, and children.
3. **Find the Edmond–Verna civil or church marriage record.** It could establish date/place, residences, occupations, witnesses, and exact names.
4. **Inspect Edmond's 1920, 1930, 1940, and 1950 census images.** These could supply residence chronology, schooling, language, occupation, marital timing, and household members.
5. **Obtain the Fall 2009 *Terrebonne Life Lines*, volume 28, page 181.** The shared index entries suggest an article specifically about Aubin Vincent's family, potentially resolving Edmond's sibling list and biographical details.
6. **Inspect Aubin's 1887 baptism and Aubin/Lena marriage record.** These should upgrade the link to Quentin/Jeanne and may identify godparents or witnesses from the extended family.
7. **Obtain Quentin's baptism, marriage, death, or succession record.** This is the critical evidence needed to verify the link to François Luc Théodore and Céleste Félonise.
8. **Obtain the 1846 François Luc Théodore–Céleste Félonise marriage entry and the Spring 1989 *Life Lines* article.** These may give parents and places directly.
9. **Inspect the original 1825–1826 François Buquet land acts and François Michel's succession/death/marriage records.** These may confirm identity, spouse, heirs, signature, and French origin.
10. **Search French records only after a Louisiana record narrows François Michel's origin.** A specific commune or parents' names are needed before responsible French parish research; do not default to Saint-Malo.
11. **Trace Jeanne Savoie and Céleste LeBlanc independently if the broader Acadian ancestry is pursued.** Require a documented parent-child chain before naming a pre-deportation ancestor.
