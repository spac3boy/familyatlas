### La Bergère identity reasoning

The transcribed embarkation list reports family 55 as Charles Dugas, age 45, a pit sawyer, with sons Jean Charles, age 20; Pierre Olivier, age 18; and Joseph, age 16; and daughters Marie Joseph and Marguerite. The ship left France on 14 May 1785 and reached Louisiana on 15 August after 93 days. A separate genealogy reports Joseph Simon Dugas born 5 January 1769 in Brittany to Charles Dugas and Marguerite Granger. A 16-year-old Joseph in Charles's household is chronologically and relationally consistent with Joseph Simon. This makes the identification `probable`, not verified: the archive has a transcription and converging genealogy, not the original passenger image plus an unbroken set of original parentage records.

The research further places Joseph Simon's birth in France during the Acadian exile period and associates the family with the Great Upheaval. Abraham Dugas is placed among early Acadian settlers by the early 1640s. Those historical classifications are supported by secondary/compiled sources and remain subordinate to the confidence of the lineage connecting them to Rita.

## Biographical narrative

Rita LeBlanc Comeaux was born on 20 April 1928 and was described in her obituary as a native of Cankton who lived most of her life in Carencro. She died at Evangeline Oaks Guest House in Carencro on 13 February 2017 and was interred at St. Peter Catholic Cemetery. The obituary identifies five children, eleven grandchildren, her sister Rella, and her parents Lucuis LeBlanc and Euchariste Dugas Racca.

The source tasks corrected an important earlier risk: Philomene Comeaux LeBlanc was not Rita's biological mother. Philomene's own obituary identifies Rita as her stepdaughter and Rella as her daughter. The combined evidence makes Rella a probable paternal half-sister, but that biological half-sibling characterization remains an inference until direct birth records are inspected.

The most extensive researched ancestry is through Euchariste Dugas. Compiled profiles, transcribed church citations, Acadian genealogies, passenger-list material, and census transcriptions create a coherent probable line through Moise J. Dugas and Emma Miller to Joseph Simon Dugas, the Dugas family aboard La Bergère in 1785, and earlier Dugas households at Port Royal. Exact seventeenth-century dates are less secure than the reported relationships. Rita's paternal LeBlanc ancestry stops at Lucuis because no candidate parents could be attached responsibly.

## Conflicts and discrepancies

1. **Biological mother versus stepmother.** Philomene Comeaux LeBlanc was a plausible but incorrect maternal assignment because she was Lucuis's wife and Rella's mother. Rita's obituary names Euchariste Dugas Racca as Rita's mother, and Philomene's obituary explicitly calls Rita a stepdaughter. Preferred conclusion: Euchariste was the biological mother; Philomene was the stepmother.
2. **Rella's exact sibling type.** Rita's obituary says sister; Philomene's says Rella was her daughter and Rita her stepdaughter. Shared father Lucuis makes paternal half-sister the strongest inference, but no direct birth records were inspected. Retain `probable`.
3. **Rita's middle name.** Michael believed she probably had none; a compiled tree uses `Rita M. LeBlanc`. Neither establishes the legal name. Canonical name omits the initial; question remains unresolved.
4. **Priscilla's surname and spouse reporting.** Philomene's 2015 obituary calls her `Priscilla LeBlanc` and names husband Karlon; Rita's 2017 obituary calls her `Priscilla C. Babineaux` and names husband Tippy LeBlanc. The notices may describe different points in a relationship/name history, but that chronology was not researched. Both reports are retained without assuming why they differ.
5. **LeBlanc capitalization.** Michael and the project use `LeBlanc`; obituaries often use `Leblanc`. Both are retained as documentary forms; no distinct identity is implied.
6. **Lucuis spelling.** Searches considered Lucuis, Lucius, Louis, and Lucien. Rita's obituary and a later Cankton parish memorial support `Lucuis`, so it is retained as canonical for her father. This does not identify his parents.
7. **Emma Boudreaux's given name.** FamilySearch labels Adolphe Miller's wife `Josephine Boudreaux`; other compilations use `Emma Boudreaux`, and St. Charles Borromeo-derived material uses `Marie Emma Boudreaux`. The best working conclusion is Marie Emma/Emma, but the given name remains `probable` until the original church record is inspected. `Josephine` is retained as the conflicting value.
8. **Early Dugas dates.** Compiled sources vary for people in the 1600s—for example Claude's birth appears around 1649 or 1652, and Abraham's death appears as 1698 or more cautiously before 1700. Relationship claims are treated as firmer than exact dates.
9. **Céleste's birth year.** The source synthesis used circa 1779; a recovered compiled page gives circa 1778. The packet preserves `circa 1778/1779` rather than choosing an unsupported exact year.
10. **Euchariste's Racca surname.** The surname may reflect a later marriage, but the spouse and event were not identified. It must not be transformed into an unnamed or guessed marriage fact.

## Rejected hypotheses and false leads

### Lucius John LeBlanc (1893–1951)

This candidate was considered because his given name, surname, and generation could appear to fit Rita's father. He is rejected. His profile places him in Montegut, Terrebonne Parish, identifies his 1913 marriage to Florence Oraline Cloutier, and continues his association with Terrebonne Parish and New Orleans. That family, marriage, and geography do not match the Cankton/Carencro family centered on Lucuis, Euchariste, Philomene, Rita, and Rella. Do not attach Arthur LeBlanc, Marie Eve Chauvin, or Florence Oraline Cloutier to Rita's line.

### Plaisance-to-Canary-Islands hypothesis

Research found a potentially interesting Placencia/Plaisance line in Louisiana that might lead to Canary Islands ancestry behind Marie Ruffin Plaisance. The crucial parent-child connection to Marie Ruffin was not established. The hypothesis is left unattached and must not appear as Rita's ancestry.

### Racca spouse inference

The form Euchariste Dugas Racca suggests a later used surname and possibly a later marriage. No defensible spouse identity or marriage record was found. No Racca husband is added.

### Surname history as pedigree proof

The Acadian character of the Dugas surname and Acadiana geography was not used by itself to extend the tree. The historical conclusion is attached only through the preserved probable parent-child chain. General surname history is not proof of Rita's specific descent.
