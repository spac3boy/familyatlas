## Biographical narrative

Michael supplied two foundational facts: Verna Buquet was the wife of his paternal grandfather Edmond and was originally from Spring Grove, Minnesota. Independent obituary evidence identifies her as Verna Arlene Bakke Buquet, born in Spring Grove on 18 January 1921 to Oscar Bakke and Olga Doely Bakke. It records three children, including Aubin Buquet, and places her in Houma, Louisiana, late in life. Her movement from Minnesota to Louisiana is therefore real, but its timing and circumstances remain unresolved.

Verna's best-established individual accomplishment is her World War II service. Her obituary states that she served in the United States Navy as a registered nurse, while the veterans-burial index records her rank as lieutenant. No personnel file or station assignment was located, so the archive does not add unit, dates, or deployment details.

Norwegian ancestry is supported by records rather than by surname or locality alone. Oscar's transcribed Minnesota death register identifies his parents as Martin Bakke and Olava Duklet. Martin's 1938 death-register transcription identifies his parents as Hans Hansen Bakke and Ingeborg Skore and reports both as Norway-born. A Spring Grove settler history describes Hans as born in Ringebu, Gudbrandsdalen, immigrating in 1840, living first in Wisconsin, and later settling west of Spring Grove. The same history and census material place Olava's origins in Verdal and her immigration about 1870. The maternal Doely line is less complete: record evidence establishes the Nicolai Doely–Serina Roble household and the older Ingvald Doely–Helene Blexrud couple, but the parent-child bridges to Olga and Nicolai still rely on compiled genealogy and remain probable.

Spring Grove's status as an early Norwegian-American community is historical context, not lineage proof. The ancestry case rests on the named parents, Norway-born statements, locations of origin, and immigration narrative tied to specific ancestors.

## Conflicts and discrepancies

1. **Oscar's birth year: 1884 versus 1885.** The 1937 Houston County death-register transcription gives 31 January 1884 and age 52 years, 11 months, 10 days. The recovered FamilySearch profile gives 31 January 1885 and age 51 at death. The original research explicitly preferred the contemporary Minnesota register, but because only a transcription was inspected, this packet retains 1884 as the preferred probable value and preserves 1885 as the conflict.
2. **Martin's birth year: 1854 versus 1855 (and a compiled 1853 value).** The 1938 death-register transcription gives 1 April 1854. A historical biographical account/compiled material uses 1 April 1855, and some compiled profiles display 1853. The death-register value is preferred, but the exact year should be resolved from a Wisconsin birth/baptism record or original death image.
3. **Olava's dates and name.** The research uses about 1853 or about 1855 for birth and 1928 for death, with surname forms `Dukleth`, `Duklet`, `Duklith`, and `Dukleith`. These may reflect Norwegian-American spelling/transcription rather than different people, but no original record set was inspected to normalize them.
4. **Serine versus Serina; Roble versus Robble.** The 1900 death-register transcription uses `Serina Robble`; compiled genealogy uses `Serine/Serina Roble`. Both forms are preserved. No maiden-name or Norwegian parish record establishes a canonical original spelling.
5. **Nicolai versus Nicoli.** Compiled genealogy uses Nicolai Ingvaldsen Doely; the child's death transcription uses Nicoli Doely. Both describe the same probable father in the working family, but an original record is needed for the preferred spelling.
6. **Doely spellings and geographic origin.** `Doely`, `Doley`, and `Doli` appear in local/compiled material. The original research suggested an Akershus-area origin for Doely/Blexrud, but did not prove a precise place. It remains unresolved.
7. **Ingeborg's surname and locality.** Martin's death transcription names Ingeborg Skore and says she was Norway-born; the settler history associates her with Tinn, Telemark. No original Norwegian record confirms spelling or parents.
8. **Verna's death place.** Her obituary calls her a Houma resident but does not say she died in Houma. Residence is not converted into death place.
9. **Burial timing.** The obituary announced that burial would follow the 23 January service; the veterans index confirms the cemetery but not the exact interment date. The cemetery is verified, while the exact date is unresolved.
10. **Two Michael Buquets in the obituary.** One appears among Verna's children and another among her grandchildren. The archive reference person is the grandson according to Michael's family statement. The two identities must never be merged.

## Rejected hypotheses and false leads

### Norwegian ancestry inferred only from `Bakke` or Spring Grove

The surname and locality suggested a promising research direction, but they are not evidence of a specific lineage. Norwegian ancestry is accepted only where named records identify Norway-born ancestors or specific Norwegian origins. A generic claim based solely on surname/location is rejected.
