# Family geography and migration archive

## Archive metadata

- **Archive status:** reviewed
- **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-04
- **Scope:** Every meaningful place associated with an accepted or probable member of Michael Buquet's maternal or paternal family in the completed A1–A7 research packets, plus a limited exclusion register for geographic false leads that could otherwise be mistaken for family places
- **Purpose:** Research-preservation layer for eventual journey and place normalization. This is not application data and contains no map coordinates or map code.
- **Evidence boundary:** Prepared from all completed files under `research/people/` and `research/branches/`, following `research/README.md` and `research/chat-inventory.md`. Geographic claims vulnerable to conflation were checked against the original Family History project chats `Explain Surname History` (`6a89bd6f-ac54-83ea-b46b-e1868c2089ba`) and `Continue Rita Research` (`6a9af740-124c-83ea-8fa8-f79c259cff72`). No new internet research was conducted for A8.

## Conventions

- Place IDs are **proposed research IDs**, not canonical application records. They use `place-` plus lower-case ASCII geographic components.
- Confidence uses only `verified`, `probable`, and `unresolved`.
- A person's association with a place inherits the confidence of the identity and relationship chain. Thus an accurately transcribed place can still be only `probable` as part of Michael's genealogy.
- Modern administrative parents are recorded where the completed packets identify them. `not established in archive` is used instead of silently supplying a county, parish, province, or modern equivalent.
- `Carenco` is retained as Michael's original family wording, but `Carencro` is the modern form used by the independent records.
- A town name does not imply a street address, household, hospital, cemetery, or church. Exact sites are separate entries.

### Precision vocabulary

| Precision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **exact documented location** | A specifically named church, cemetery, facility, farm description, waterway bank, or historical settlement appears in the evidence. Exact coordinates or an address may still be unknown. |
| **town/city-level location** | The settlement is known, but no exact site or address is established. |
| **parish/county-level location** | Only the administrative area is established. |
| **state/province/region-level location** | Evidence does not support a narrower locality. |
| **country-level location** | Only the country is established. |
| **probable location** | The place is supported by compiled, derivative, secondary, or family evidence but lacks sufficient original-record confirmation. |
| **location mentioned without enough precision** | The source names an institution, region, or broad place without enough information to place it safely at a narrower level. |

## Geographic conflicts and unresolved precision

1. **Dulac and Houma:** They are not competing birth/death claims for Edmond. Dulac is Michael's undated “from” statement; Houma is the death index's last residence.
2. **Carenco and Carencro:** `Carenco` is preserved as family wording. Independent sources use `Carencro`; no separate place is created.
3. **Spring Grove city, township, and area:** These are not interchangeable. Verna's birth and Martin's 1938 death use the settlement; Oscar's 1895 township residence is only a compiled lead; the Hans farm was described as four miles west of Spring Grove.
4. **Muskego/Racine County area:** The settler history's geographic wording is retained without reconstructing nineteenth-century county boundaries. The exact land parcel and modern administrative parent remain unresolved in this archive.
5. **France versus Saint-Malo:** France is the narrowest defensible origin for François Michel Jacques. Saint-Malo was explicitly retracted and remains only in the exclusion register.
6. **Port Royal/Acadia modern equivalence:** The research established the historical names, not a modern administrative conversion. No modern place is silently substituted.
7. **Akershus:** This is only an area-level Doely/Blexrud research lead, not a birthplace, parish, farm, or migration endpoint.
8. **Beau Sejours:** The institution is named, but its locality and facility type were not established in the preserved source excerpt.
9. **Magnolia Cemetery:** The cemetery is documented within the Terrebonne source set, but its exact settlement and individual plots were not established. It must not be used to infer Edmond's burial.
10. **Louisiana arrival points:** Neither the *La Bergère* transcription nor the François Michel research establishes a specific Louisiana landing port or first residence.
