# Standard research-packet template

This template is the required shape for person-level research packets in this archive. Omit no section; write `Not found` or `Unresolved` instead of guessing.

## Packet metadata

- **Stable person ID:** `person-given-middle-surname[-disambiguator]`
- **Packet status:** draft | reviewed | research-exhausted
- **Last reviewed:** YYYY-MM-DD
- **Scope:** person, immediate family, and relevant ancestral links
- **Evidence boundary:** describe what material was reviewed and what was not accessible

## Migration and movement

Classify each entry as:

- **Documented move:** a source explicitly describes the move.
- **Inferred movement:** evidence strongly indicates relocation, but no source states the move.
- **Locations at different dates:** both endpoints are known, but the route and move are not.

## Biographical narrative

A short evidence-grounded narrative. Label inference in the prose and do not convert family tradition into established fact.

## Claim register

| Claim ID | Claim | Confidence | Evidence/source IDs | Reasoning/limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Every important genealogical claim must appear here or in an equivalent event/relationship table with one of exactly three states:

- **verified:** directly supported by a strong record or by multiple independent records whose identity match is secure.
- **probable:** evidence favors the claim, but a direct record or crucial identity link is missing.
- **unresolved:** the archive cannot choose responsibly among possibilities, or no supporting evidence was located.

## Source-by-source evidence

| Source ID | Full citation/title | Record type/date | Repository/database | URL or preserved citation handle | Evidence type | Claims supported | Reliability/conflicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Evidence type should be one of: original record/image, derivative index/transcription, compiled genealogy, secondary history, obituary, or family-provided information.

## Conflicts and discrepancies

Preserve every material disagreement in names, dates, places, relationships, or identity. State the preferred interpretation only when the evidence supports one, and retain the rejected value.

## Rejected hypotheses and false leads

Name the candidate or hypothesis, show why it was considered, show why it was rejected or left unattached, and cite the evidence. Rejected people must never enter production genealogy as relatives.

## Research already attempted

List databases, repositories, name variants, date/place combinations, image searches, and archival indexes already checked. Distinguish an exhausted open-web search from an exhausted archival search.

## Recommendations for future research

Prioritize specific records and repositories. Explain what claim each record could prove or disprove.
